The problem

Why the restaurant WhatsApp goes unanswered

A restaurant's WhatsApp is busiest exactly when the team is busiest. At 7:45 PM someone asks if you have a table for four at 8, someone else wants to know if the biryani is spicy, and a regular wants to order pickup. The host is seating guests and the manager is on the floor. By the time anyone looks, the table request is an hour old and the guest has gone elsewhere.

The questions themselves are simple and repetitive: are you open, where are you, is there parking, do you have vegan options, can I book the terrace. They don't need a person; they need an accurate answer from your menu and your hours. A person is needed for a smaller set of moments, and the system should know which ones.

What it looks like

A reservation with an allergy

The allergy is the line that matters. The assistant answers from the menu information you give it, but you tell it which questions must always go to a person, and serious allergies are the obvious one. It hands the chat over with an alert, and your manager replies from the shared inbox.

Orders

Direct orders on WhatsApp, alongside delivery apps

WhatsApp automation doesn't replace delivery apps. It handles the guests who message you directly, often your regulars, who'd rather order from you than through a platform. A typical direct-order flow:

  1. The guest asks for the menu or names what they want; the assistant answers prices and options from your menu.
  2. It collects the items, any notes, the name, and either a pickup time or a delivery address in your delivery area.
  3. The order goes to your team to confirm, with a push alert, so the kitchen decides timing, not the bot.
  4. If you take card payments by link, it can share your payment link; otherwise the order is marked for payment on pickup or delivery.

Around that, we can build Custom Automation for the back of house: orders written into a Google Sheet for the kitchen, a daily summary of direct orders, or a feedback message the morning after a reservation.

Bringing guests back

Regulars, quiet nights and seasonal menus

Every guest who messages you becomes a contact you can reach again. With paced broadcasts you can announce a new menu, an Iftar or Suhoor set, a brunch, or a quiet-night offer to your list, without blasting it from a phone. On the official API, these marketing messages use Meta-approved templates. Timed follow-ups can go to guests who asked about an event or a large booking and didn't confirm, and they stop as soon as the guest replies.

When the menu or hours change, you edit them in the dashboard and the assistant answers from the new version. Guests can also send a voice note, which is transcribed, and the same assistant can answer your Instagram DMs (see Instagram DM automation).

OrcaFlo connects to the WhatsApp Business number you already use, in one of two ways. Meta's official WhatsApp Business API gives you the verified route; Meta bills its own per-message fees to your account, and messages sent more than 24 hours after a customer's last message need a Meta-approved template. A QR link to the WhatsApp Business app is quicker to set up and has no Meta fees, but it carries no green tick and some ban risk, because WhatsApp doesn't officially allow unofficial apps. We'll recommend one on the call. OrcaFlo's price is the same on both.
